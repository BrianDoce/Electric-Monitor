# Grid insights - 2026-10-15

**Oct 15 peak demand came in ~6,700 MWh below what a 91.9°F day would normally drive**

- **What:** Peak demand was 31,331 MWh against 38,074 MWh expected for the day's max temperature, a shortfall of about 6,743 MWh (~18%).
- **How unusual:** z = -2.50 against the rest of the ~30-day window. The peak was also below the window's average peak of 35,387 MWh, even though the day was ~4°F hotter than the 87.7°F window mean.
- **Likely driver (temperature signal):** The single max-temperature figure may not reflect conditions across the load base. Heat that was localized or brief would lift max temp without a matching rise in cooling load. This is a likely explanation; the data does not confirm it.
- **Likely driver (solar):** Solar supplied 30.7% of the mix and renewables 55.5%, slightly above the 53.9% window mean. If metered grid demand excludes rooftop generation, strong sun could suppress measured peak on a hot day. This is a possible contributor, not confirmed.
- **Data note:** "Other" shows -3.2% of the mix. This could reflect net storage charging, imports or an accounting adjustment. It is worth checking that it isn't distorting the demand figure.

**Watch next:** Check whether the low demand-vs-temperature residual persists on the following hot days. If it does, that points to a structural shift such as solar or a temperature-proxy issue rather than a one-off. If it doesn't, check the Oct 15 data for errors.
